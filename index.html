D.S. Nelson Research

Syntropy Systems Labs

Independent research investigating how constraints shape the behavior, stability, emergence, and failure of complex informational systems. The Constrained Informational Systems (CIS) research program develops structural, formal, and empirical methods for analyzing constraint architecture, admissibility, verification, authority, and regime change across informational systems.

It contains public research notes, conceptual provenance materials, supporting documentation, and versioned research releases. Detailed internal research artifacts are maintained separately in a private research corpus.

Current Research Directions

Current CIS research extends the foundational and architectural work of the first two preprints toward formalization, empirical evaluation, and multi-system analysis.

  • Constraint Composition and Topology: Investigating how constraints compose, interact, conflict, propagate, and change system behavior across architectural layers, including the mathematical structures required to represent these relationships.
  • Semantic Entropy and Basin Stability: Developing candidate measures for semantic state, stability, admissibility, and regime transition in constrained informational systems, with future computational modeling and simulation planned.
  • Empirical CIS Evaluation: Developing governed experimental designs for evaluating CIS propositions, including constraint-location effects, regime transitions, and other falsifiable consequences of the framework.
  • Multi-Agent Constraint Architectures: Exploring how communication topology, shared state, constraint propagation, verification, provenance, and authority interact across multi-agent systems.
